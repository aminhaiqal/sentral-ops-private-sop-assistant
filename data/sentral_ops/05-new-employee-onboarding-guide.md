# New Employee Onboarding Guide

**Owner:** HR/Admin Coordinator  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Last Reviewed:** 2026-05-14  
**Classification:** Internal Use Only  

## Purpose

This guide gives new employees a structured start at Sentral Ops. It focuses on practical operating knowledge, system discipline, escalation habits, and data handling expectations.

## First-Week Priorities

In week one, every new employee should learn:

- What Sentral Ops sells and which customer segments we serve.
- The difference between Sales, Operations, Customer Support, Finance/Admin, and Management responsibilities.
- Where approved SOPs, customer templates, price references, and issue logs are stored.
- How to record customer interactions clearly.
- What information must be checked before escalating an issue.
- What data must not be copied into personal devices, public AI tools, or unapproved channels.
- Who owns common decisions such as refund approval, delivery delay escalation, credit notes, and stock substitution.

New support staff should sit with an experienced Support Lead for live case review before replying independently to complex customer complaints.

## System Access

System access is requested by the hiring manager and approved by the function owner. New employees should receive only the access required for their role. Access to finance records, customer credit terms, and management approvals is restricted.

Shared passwords are not allowed. If access is missing, the employee should report it to the hiring manager rather than borrowing another staff member's account.

## Role-Specific Onboarding

Support employees should learn order lookup, delivery issue triage, missing item checks, wrong item handling, customer update templates, and escalation notes.

Operations employees should learn picking and packing checks, route notes, stock exception recording, partial delivery rules, failed delivery recovery, and driver communication standards.

Finance/Admin employees should learn invoice creation triggers, required invoice information, payment follow-up schedule, credit note handling, and payment confirmation rules.

Sales employees should learn account notes, approved pricing process, recurring order patterns, customer expectation setting, and when to involve Operations or Finance/Admin.

## First 30 Days Plan

During the first 30 days, new employees should complete supervised case practice, review key SOPs, shadow at least one cross-functional workflow, and complete a manager check-in on escalation judgment.

By the end of the first month, employees should know where to find approved process documents and should avoid relying only on chat history or informal memory.

## Escalation Rules

New employees should escalate when:

- A customer asks for refund, credit note, cancellation, special pricing, or manager review.
- A delivery is at risk, delayed beyond the promised window, failed, or partial without prior agreement.
- The issue involves payment confirmation, disputed invoice, overdue account, or credit hold.
- The product substitution affects hygiene, safety, regulated use, brand-specific requirements, or customer approval.
- The employee is unsure whether the SOP applies.

## Data Handling Rules

Customer information, invoices, payment details, staff records, internal pricing, and supplier terms must remain in approved company systems. Public AI tools, personal email, personal cloud storage, and personal messaging accounts are not approved for confidential company data.

## AI Assistant Notes

The assistant may explain onboarding priorities, role-specific learning areas, first-month expectations, and escalation rules. It must not grant system access, decide employment matters, expose employee records, or replace manager onboarding review.
