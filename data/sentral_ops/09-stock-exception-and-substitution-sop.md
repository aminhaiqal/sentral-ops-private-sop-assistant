# Stock Exception and Substitution SOP

**Owner:** Operations Manager  
**Version:** 1.3  
**Last Reviewed:** 2026-05-17  
**Classification:** Internal Use Only  

## Purpose

This SOP defines how Sentral Ops handles stock exceptions and product substitution. The aim is to keep customer operations moving without creating safety, compliance, billing, or trust issues.

## Stock Exception Types

Out of stock means the ordered item is not available for picking at the time required.

Short pick means only part of the ordered quantity is available.

Supplier delay means replenishment is expected later than planned.

Discontinued item means the original item is no longer available from the supplier or has been replaced.

Quality hold means stock exists but cannot be released because of damage, expiry concern, packaging issue, or supplier quality notice.

## Initial Checks

Operations must check the approved inventory or purchasing channel, open purchase orders, customer order priority, possible equivalent items, and whether the customer has brand or specification restrictions.

Support may ask for an update, but Operations owns the stock exception record.

## Low-Risk Substitution

Low-risk substitution may be approved by Operations when the item is functionally equivalent, not safety-sensitive, not hygiene-sensitive, not customer-branded, and not materially different in price or specification.

Examples include similar office pens, comparable general-purpose cloths, equivalent bin liners of the same size and strength, and standard pantry supplies where brand is not specified.

The customer should still be informed when the delivered item differs from the original item.

## High-Risk Substitution

High-risk substitution requires customer approval before dispatch and may require manager approval. High-risk items include:

- Clinic gloves and hygiene-sensitive consumables.
- Chemical products or cleaning agents with different active ingredients.
- Safety items such as masks, footwear, signage, or protective equipment.
- Food-contact packaging.
- Size-specific, machine-specific, or customer-branded items.
- Items linked to special pricing or contract requirements.

Clinic gloves must not be substituted without customer approval because material, size, powder-free status, intended use, and customer preference may matter.

## Substitution Approval Matrix

Low-risk substitution with same or lower price may be approved by Operations Coordinator with Support notification.

Low-risk substitution with higher price requires Operations Manager approval and clear billing instruction.

High-risk substitution requires customer approval before dispatch. If the customer cannot approve in time, Operations should offer backorder, split delivery, or cancellation of affected line where appropriate.

Emergency high-risk exceptions require Operations Manager approval and written customer confirmation as soon as practical.

## Customer Templates

Substitution request:

"The original item is currently unavailable. We can offer the following alternative if it is suitable for your use: [item]. Please confirm before we dispatch, as it differs from the original item."

Backorder option:

"The original item is not available for immediate dispatch. We can hold the line for the next replenishment or deliver the available items first. Please confirm your preference."

## Invoice and Pricing Rules

Substitution must be reflected correctly on the invoice or delivery note. If the substitute is lower priced, Finance/Admin should bill the lower price unless a contract says otherwise. If the substitute is higher priced, the customer must agree to the price or the manager must approve a documented service recovery decision.

## AI Assistant Notes

The assistant may explain stock exception types, substitution risk levels, approval requirements, and customer templates. It must not confirm live stock availability or approve a substitution. For clinic gloves, the assistant should state that customer approval is required before substitution.
